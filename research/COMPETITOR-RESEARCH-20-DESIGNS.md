# Product 001 — Competitive Research: 20-Design T-Shirt/POD Bundles

Research date: 2026-09-25

## Scope

Current Etsy market research for digital T-shirt/POD design bundles, with special attention to products containing about 20 designs, PNG/SVG deliverables, pricing, presentation, licensing language, and visible shop traction.

Important limitation: Etsy generally exposes **shop-level sales**, not reliable per-listing unit sales. Therefore shop sales must NOT be presented as sales of the individual bundle. Revenue figures for competitors are not asserted unless directly disclosed by the seller; simple price × shop-sales calculations would be misleading.

## Additional market context

Current Etsy search results also show a very aggressive low-price/high-volume segment:
- 1,200 streetwear designs around $2.03 sale price.
- 5,000+ streetwear designs around $1.30.
- 4 million+ T-shirt designs around $0.91.
- 1,500+ streetwear designs around $2.95.
- 70+ streetwear designs around $10.20.

These are not direct substitutes for a curated 20-design premium bundle, but they establish the pressure from mega-bundles and low-price sellers.

## What competitors are doing

### 1. Quantity is heavily used as the value proposition
The market contains both:
- cheap 20-design bundles around $2–4;
- premium-positioned 20-design bundles around $5–10;
- enormous 70–5000+ design bundles below or around $10.

Therefore quantity alone cannot justify our price.

### 2. File-format stacking is common
Competitors frequently provide combinations such as:
- PNG
- SVG
- EPS
- DXF
- JPG
- PDF

Our baseline should remain PNG + SVG because these are directly useful for POD and vector workflows. Extra formats should only be added if they improve customer value and can be reliably produced and QA-tested.

### 3. 300 DPI + transparent PNG is a market expectation
Multiple current competitors explicitly advertise 300 DPI and transparent backgrounds.

### 4. Mockup/presentation quality matters
Current listings increasingly use:
- full collection preview grids;
- multiple garment mockups;
- close-up design previews;
- lifestyle streetwear photography;
- explicit badges for file count and formats.

### 5. Commercial-use language varies substantially
Some sellers allow physical products for sale but prohibit raw-file redistribution/resale. Others are personal-use only. We need a precise license that clearly states what buyers may and may not do.

### 6. Originality is a useful commercial differentiator
Some sellers explicitly market original artwork and/or no-AI artwork. We should make originality and professional art direction part of the product story without making unsupported claims.

## Price positioning for Product 001

Our fixed production target is:

**20 original designs**
**PNG 300 DPI + SVG**
**professional collection presentation**
**POD-ready**
**professional mockups**
**clear license**
**ZIP packaging**
**$6–7 target price**

Recommended initial price architecture for testing:
- regular target: **$6.99**
- launch/promotion test: **$5.99**
- avoid positioning below $3 unless the product is deliberately moved into the mass-budget segment.

This is a positioning decision, not a forecast of sales.

## Design direction derived from research

The 20 designs should NOT be 20 generic text-only quotes.

The collection should combine:
- strong typography;
- original illustration/graphic hooks;
- consistent visual language;
- enough variation that each design can stand alone;
- strong thumbnail readability;
- designs that work on both light and dark garments where technically appropriate;
- a clear niche identity.

For Product 001, the current coffee/humor direction remains the working concept, but the 20-design collection must be re-evaluated for differentiation before all artwork is produced.

## What we must NOT copy

Competitor phrases, characters, exact compositions, distinctive layouts, logos, protected brands, celebrity likenesses, or other protected expression must not be reproduced.

Competitor listings are market references only.

## Data integrity rule

Do not convert shop-level sales into listing-level sales.

Do not claim competitor revenue without direct evidence.

Do not use competitor sales as a forecast for our product.
